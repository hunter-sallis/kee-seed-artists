Execution board

How it worksThe stages, what each detail means, and how to keep your tracking safe

The stages

  1. Not started → Followed

    Follow and like two recent posts.

  2. Followed → Engaged

    Comment on the newest post or reply to a story, so the name is familiar before a message lands.

  3. Engaged → Message sent

    One direct message. Mention their top song or new release and offer one beat in that lane, not a pack. The board sets a follow-up date five days out.

  4. Replied → Session booked → Song made

    Send the beat, offer a date, make the record, then plan the release and post the cook-up clip.

  5. Pass

    Use it for anyone who is not a fit. They stay on the board but drop out of Today.

Your tracking

Stages, dates, notes and any handle you type are stored in this browser on this device. They are not sent anywhere and nobody else can see them. They will not appear on another phone or laptop, and clearing browser data removes them, so save a backup regularly.

What each detail means

  1. Latest release

    The newest release on the Spotify profile, with its exact date. “Dropped” appears when it is 30 days old or less. 40 of 106 had one on September 28, 2026. The board works out the age from today’s date, but it does not re-read Spotify, so anything released after September 28, 2026 is not shown.

  2. Label

    The label line Spotify shows on that release. “Self-released” means the line is the artist’s own name or a distributor default: 52 of 106. Otherwise the line is shown as written: 54. A named line can still be the artist’s own company, so treat it as a question to ask, not proof of a deal.

  3. Mgmt

    Read from the public Instagram bio only. 20 name management. “Not visible” means the profile is hidden from logged-out visitors. The manager’s handle is on the artist’s profile; it is not repeated here.

  4. Instagram

    98 of 106 have a handle. 82 were published by the artist (Spotify profile or bio, or a SoundCloud or YouTube page they control). 16 were found by search and are marked “check first”. 8 have none yet; type one in under Details.

Where the list came from

  1. Start from Dxrtball

    Dxrtball’s Spotify profile is named “2Sdxrt3all” and had 82,293 monthly listeners on September 28, 2026. That figure is the size limit, so nobody here is bigger than he is.

  2. Walk sideways

    We read his “Fans also like” list (40 artists), then the lists of the 23 neighbours at or under his size: 24 lists in all. 194 artists measured, 89 kept.

  3. Rank

    Points for being in Dxrtball’s own list, for how many of the 24 lists name the artist, for audience size, for a recent release, and for a confirmed Atlanta-area home base. 60 or more is “Bring on now”, 45 to 59 is “Warm up”, under 45 is “Watch”.

  4. Walk Atlanta

    We then read the “Fans also like” lists of 16 Atlanta artists and producers (169 profiles) and researched where each new name is based. 17 Atlanta names that were not on the first list were added as “Atlanta seeds”. Atlanta rank uses proof of home base, how many of the 16 Atlanta lists name them, how recent the latest release is, and audience size.

  5. Keep rappers and singers on the main ranking

    Producers, DJs and tape hosts, archive and repost pages, duplicate profiles and catalogs with no release after 2023 were left off. Atlanta producers appear only on the Atlanta tab.
